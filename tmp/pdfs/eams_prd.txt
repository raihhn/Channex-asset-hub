EAMS PRD v2.0 | Internal Product Baseline
1. Executive Summary
EAMS is a centralized asset lifecycle management system for event and retail assets. It tracks assets from creation 
through storage, deployment, transfer, maintenance, reuse, rebuild, recycling, and retirement.
The system uses two connected platforms: Web Dashboard as the internal control and management layer, and Mobile as 
the field execution layer used by internal teams and vendors.
The original PRD defines the product around real-time availability, lifecycle monitoring, requests, recycling, and external 
vendor visibility. This v2.0 preserves those foundations and adds the operational rules refined during product design, 
especially transfer history, role-scoped visibility, photo evidence, condition inspection, master-data ownership, and 
component reuse.
2. Product Goals
 Real-time visibility: know what asset exists, which brand owns it, where it is, its current status, and who is responsible.
 Lifecycle tracking: preserve the complete operational history of each asset.
 Controlled handover: every movement creates a traceable record with destination, responsible party, condition, 
evidence, and approval where required.
 Condition evidence: use standardized photos and structured damage records instead of free-text-only status.
 Role-based access: internal users and vendor users see different levels of history and management data.
 Reuse and recycling: distinguish between repair, reuse, rebuild, recycling, and retirement.

5. Asset Architecture
5.1 Core Categories
 Booth: Current Booth, Modular Booth (1x1 / 2x2 / 3x3), Reuse / Retired.
 POSM: banners, standees, shelf talkers, display boards and related point-of-sale materials.
 Inventories / Supporting Assets: furniture, props, lighting, technology equipment, consumables and other operational 
items.
5.2 Component / Parent-Child Structure
A composite asset may contain reusable components. This is required for cases such as a booth where only the chairs 
and tables are reused while the main structure stays in storage or is rebuilt.
Example: Booth Colourverse -> Structure / Counter / Table / Chair x4 / Lighting.
Each child component can carry its own status, location, condition, and movement history while remaining linked to the 
parent asset.

 Events are created and maintained by Internal users in Web.
 Stores are created and maintained by Internal users in Web.
 Warehouses are created and maintained by Internal users in Web.
 Internal PIC profiles are created and maintained by Internal users in Web.

11. Photo System
Photos are evidence, not decoration. Initial asset photos and later condition photos are preserved as separate records.
11.1 Initial photos
 Minimum: front / side / detail.
 Unlimited additional photos allowed.
 Never overwritten by later condition photos.
 Accessible through a full-screen gallery.
11.2 Condition photos
 Minimum required photo types: front / side / detail.
 Unlimited additional photos.
 Photo types: front, side, detail, damage, other.
 Each photo stores timestamp, uploader, type, and condition session.
12. Condition Inspection
The inspection flow is guided so users cannot accidentally submit a condition record with incomplete evidence.
Instruction shown to user: “Ambil minimal 3 foto aset: Tampak depan (full aset), Tampak samping / sudut, Detail (bagian 
penting). Tambahkan foto kerusakan jika ada.”

13. Transfer / Handover
Transfer is a core action and is used for movement to another vendor, event, store, or warehouse.
Destination selection always uses existing system records. A search field is available for fast lookup across Vendor, 
Event, Store, and Warehouse.
Step Behaviour
1. Destination Choose category and search existing destination
2. Responsible party Internal transfer may use a centrally managed PIC. Vendor 
accounts represent the vendor entity.
3. Condition Run guided condition inspection with photo evidence
4. Confirmation Review from/to, destination, responsible party, condition and 
evidence
5. Approval Applied according to configured transfer policy
6. Handover Receiving party confirms; asset state and history update

14. Approval Rules
Approval policy is centrally managed and may vary by destination, asset class, or transfer type.
 Vendor -> Vendor: internal approval required.
 Vendor -> Internal: may require internal approval according to policy.
 Internal -> Vendor: approval according to policy.
 Vendor -> Event / Store: movement is recorded and follows configured policy.
 Any approval creates an auditable decision record.
15. Asset History / Timeline
“Riwayat Aset” is a read-only lifecycle timeline. It is not the same as a “Check Condition” input form.
 Asset Created / Origin
 Transfer / Handover
 Condition Update
 Maintenance / Repair
 Request / Reservation milestones
 Reuse / Rebuild / Recycling transitions
 Approval decisions
Timeline entries are expandable. Each entry shows a concise summary first and can open into full detail, including photos 
and notes.
16. History Visibility Rules
Role History visibility
Internal
Full timeline: origin, all transfers, all conditions, all vendors, all 
PICs, all approvals, all photos, all maintenance and reuse 
records.
Vendor
Scoped timeline only: entries directly related to that vendor while 
it created, received, held, transferred, inspected, or handed over 
the asset. Hide unrelated vendor activity and internal-only PIC 
information.

22. Reuse / Recycling Pipeline
Lifecycle: Active Asset -> Flagged -> Reuse Pool -> PIC Review -> Dismantling -> Material Stock -> New Modular Asset.
 Repair: keep the same asset and restore condition.
 Reuse: deploy the same asset or component again.
 Rebuild: dismantle and create a new configuration.
 Recycle: convert into material stock.
 Retire: no further operational value.
23. Material Stock
Materials recovered from dismantling become a trackable pool that can feed future modular builds.

 Quantity

 Condition

 Available quantity
 Linked future build

31. Acceptance Criteria
AC-01: Every asset has a unique Asset ID and initial photo set.
AC-02: Initial photos remain accessible after later condition updates.
AC-03: Condition inspection requires front, side and detail photo types before submission.
AC-04: Additional photos and multiple damage entries are supported.
AC-05: Every transfer creates an immutable history entry.
AC-06: Every condition update creates an immutable history entry.
AC-07: Internal users can view full lifecycle history.
AC-08: Vendors can view only related lifecycle entries.
AC-09: Mobile destination selection uses existing master records; no manual creation.

33. Product Principles for Handover
 Do not treat the mobile app as a mini ERP.
 Do not make master data editable by vendors.
 Do not overwrite history with current state changes.
 Do not rely on free text for critical destinations.
 Do not use “condition” as a single generic status without evidence.
 Keep initial asset photos separate from latest condition photos.
 Use the Web Dashboard as the authoritative control plane.
 Use Mobile as the fast operational execution surface.
34. Design Handoff Notes
This document is intended as the baseline for generating the next Figma Make prototype and for handing the concept to 
product / engineering teams. The system should be prototyped end-to-end with realistic example data rather than generic 
placeholder Vendor A / Vendor B content.
Recommended prototype scenario: Wardah asset -> created by a vendor -> deployed to a major event -> returned to

reused / rebuilt.
